Live-session engine · Speaker = mouth · Thinker = subconscious

One brain, two parts

The persona has a fast front brain that talks and a slow subconscious that knows who it is. They are not two agents taking turns — they hear the same words at the same instant, and they share one memory. Here is the mechanism.

The anatomy

Both models receive the interviewer's speech from the first word. The Speaker is the only one with a mouth; the Thinker never speaks, it only hands up notes. What makes them one brain rather than two is the third box — a shared ledger of everything this persona has already claimed.

The sync, in milliseconds

This is the part that actually answers "how do they work in sync". The Thinker is not summoned when the Speaker gets stuck — it has been running since the interviewer's first word, speculatively, on every turn. So when the Speaker does need it, the answer is usually already in flight.

Two paths on one time axis. The only difference between them is whether the Speaker was confident enough to answer alone.

Why it has to be one brain

Two models that both improvise, with no shared memory, produce a persona that is randomly wrong. A real weak candidate is consistently wrong — they hold the same wrong belief on turn 4 and on turn 19. The ledger is what buys that consistency.

One turn, start to finish

  1. The interviewer starts talking. Audio forks at once — to the Speaker, and to a streaming transcriber whose partial text feeds the Thinker.
  2. Midway through the question the pre-gate reads that partial text and asks one deterministic question: does this probe a skill whose ceiling is low? No model involved, no latency spent.
  3. If it does, the engine tells the Speaker before its turn even begins not to answer this one alone. Getting in early is what keeps the gate off the latency path.
  4. The interviewer stops. The Speaker either answers — or plays a stall clip in the persona's own voice and waits.
  5. The Thinker's note lands, phrased as guidance rather than a script, so the Speaker's fillers and rhythm stay its own. If it misses its 700 ms deadline, the persona falls back to whatever the contract says it does under pressure.
  6. Whatever was said is written to the ledger, and a post-hoc judge reads it seconds later to flag anything that exceeded the persona's ceiling.

The Speaker never leaks an evaluation, because it is never given one. A mouth cannot say what it was never told.

Who decides what

This holds together because almost nothing is left to a model at runtime. Both models are on short leashes, and deterministic code decides when each one is allowed to matter.

The Thinker is a persona oracle, not a knowledge oracle. Asked what to say, it answers "what would this person say" — never "what is correct". For a candidate capped at level 3 in Redis, a true answer is as much a failure as an unenforced ceiling.
